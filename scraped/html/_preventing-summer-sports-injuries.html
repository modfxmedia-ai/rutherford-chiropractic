Preventing Summer Sports Injuries in Murfreesboro: Warm-Ups and Prehab

Jun 7, 2026 | Sports Injury | 0 comments

Stay Active All Summer Without Pain

Hot Middle Tennessee summers are perfect for 5Ks, early tee times, lake days, and pick-up games at the park. They are also prime time for pulled muscles, sore backs, and nagging joint pain that can shut your plans down fast. Many runners, golfers, and weekend athletes jump straight into activity without giving their bodies any real prep.

When we skip warm-ups and mobility work, the body is tighter, slower to react, and more likely to strain. That is when hamstrings grab, low backs flare, and old ankle or shoulder issues show up again. At our Murfreesboro chiropractic clinic, we talk a lot about “prehab,” or preventive rehab. The idea is simple: build resilience before pain shows up so you can enjoy the whole season, not just the first few weeks.

In this article, we will walk through practical dynamic warm-ups, joint-friendly mobility drills, and chiropractic-based prehab ideas for runners, golfers, and casual athletes. Our goal is to help you stay active, feel better, and keep your body ready for whatever your Murfreesboro summer throws at it.

Why Summer Sports Raise Your Injury Risk

Summer sports feel fun and relaxed, but your body still has to work hard. The heat, humidity, and longer days can drain you quickly. When you add in poor hydration or a rushed start, you put more stress on your joints, tendons, and nervous system than you may realize.

Common Murfreesboro summer issues include things like:

  • Ankle sprains from trail runs or uneven fields
  • Low back and hip pain after walking 18 holes or hitting at the range
  • Knee pain from court sports or pick-up games
  • Flare-ups of old auto, work, or sports injuries when activity suddenly spikes

Many people spend more time sitting during the week, then go full speed on weekends. If your spine and major joints are not moving well, summer activity only magnifies those problems. When the body is out of good alignment, certain muscles overwork while others lag behind. This imbalance makes strains, tendon irritation, and overuse injuries much more likely.

A sports injury doctor in Murfreesboro, TN pays close attention to how your body moves as a whole. We look at the spine, hips, knees, ankles, shoulders, and how they work together. When we improve alignment and movement patterns before you ramp up your summer sports, your body can handle more without breaking down.

Smart Warm-up Routines for Runners and Golfers

If you only change one thing this summer, make it your warm-up. A good warm-up should feel like a slow “ramp up” that gets your heart rate up and your joints moving through the ranges they will need for your sport.

For runners, a simple dynamic warm-up could look like:

  • 5 to 10 minutes of brisk walking or easy jogging
  • Leg swings front-to-back and side-to-side, holding a wall or fence
  • Hip circles, drawing big circles with your knee while standing
  • Walking lunges with gentle rotation through your upper body
  • Ankle mobility drills, such as slow heel-to-toe rocks and ankle circles

In hot weather, start slower than you think you need to. You want to feel warm, loose, and slightly out of breath, not exhausted, before the main part of your run.

For golfers, a golf-specific warm-up is just as important:

  • Thoracic spine rotations, hands across your chest and turn side to side
  • Shoulder circles and gentle arm swings to prep your upper body
  • Band pull-aparts to wake up the muscles between your shoulder blades
  • Hip hinges, practicing bending at the hips instead of the low back
  • Simple glute activation exercises, like mini-squats or bridges

Dynamic warm-ups use movement, not long holds. Static stretching, where you hold a stretch for longer periods, is better saved for cool-downs. Long holds before activity can actually make muscles a little “sleepy” and less responsive.

A sports injury doctor in Murfreesboro, TN can customize these warm-up steps to fit your age, current fitness, old injuries, and goals. That way you are not guessing; you are following a plan built around your body.

Mobility Drills to Protect Joints All Season

Warm-ups get your body ready in the short term. Mobility work is what keeps your joints happy all season. Certain areas take a beating with summer sports, especially ankles, hips, shoulders, and the middle of your back.

Here is why that matters. When one joint is stiff, the joints above and below are forced to move more than they should. For example, tight hips can make the low back twist and bend too much. Stiff ankles can push extra strain into the knees.

Try building in a few simple mobility drills:

  • For ankles, ankle circles and controlled calf raises off a stair or curb
  • For hips, gentle hip openers, like lying on your back and rocking your knees side to side
  • 90/90 hip transitions, sitting on the floor and rotating your hips between two positions
  • For shoulders, banded external rotations and wall slides with your arms gliding up the wall

This is part of what many coaches call a “joint-by-joint” approach. Some areas are meant to be more stable, like the knees and low back. Others should be more mobile, like the hips, ankles, and upper back. When mobility and stability are balanced, your performance often improves and your injury risk drops.

At Rutherford Spine & Wellness Center, we can assess where your personal restrictions are. Then we build short daily routines that actually fit into real life, a few minutes before bed, during a work break, or while you wait for coffee to brew.

Chiropractic-Based Prehab for Summer Athletes

Prehab simply means getting ahead of problems instead of reacting to them. It is proactive care, not just damage control. The goal is to correct movement faults, improve alignment, and strengthen weak links before they turn into pain or injury.

Chiropractic care can play a strong role in this kind of plan. Some of the ways we support prehab include:

  • Spinal and extremity adjustments to improve joint motion and alignment
  • Soft tissue therapies to ease tight muscles and irritated fascia
  • Targeted exercises for core, hips, and shoulders to build strength and control

For runners, this might include gait analysis, foot and ankle work, and hip stability exercises. For golfers, we often look closely at thoracic spine mobility, shoulder stability, and how the core handles rotation. For weekend athletes who like a bit of everything, we look at whole-body patterns, balance, and how well the body handles changes of direction.

A sports injury doctor in Murfreesboro, TN can also help you blend in rehab-style exercises, home care, and smart return-to-sport plans. The idea is not to stop what you love, but to support your body so you can keep doing it longer, with less soreness and fewer setbacks.

Make This Your Strongest Murfreesboro Summer Yet

You do not need to change your entire routine overnight. Pick one small habit to start this week. Maybe it is a 10-minute dynamic warm-up before every run, or a simple nightly mobility routine for your hips and shoulders. Small, steady changes add up fast, especially when you repeat them day after day.

At Rutherford Spine & Wellness Center, we help runners, golfers, and weekend athletes who are tired of dealing with the same aches and strains every summer. During a first visit, we take time to learn your history, check your posture and movement, talk about your sports goals, and build a prehab plan that fits your life. With the right mix of chiropractic care, focused exercises, and smart daily habits, staying active and pain-aware becomes much more realistic.

You deserve to enjoy the trails, fairways, courts, and lakes around Murfreesboro without worrying what will flare up next. When you treat your spine, joints, and nervous system with care before trouble starts, you give yourself a much better chance at a strong, pain-aware summer.
